NYC Restaurant Health Inspection Analysis

The NYC Department of Health inspects the city's ~27,000 restaurants to enforce food safety. Leadership wanted the inspection data turned into a decision tool: which restaurant types are struggling, which neighborhoods carry the most risk, and where inspection and education resources would have the greatest impact.

I cleaned and derived fields for 285,000+ inspection records in Excel, queried and modeled the data in MySQL, then built a 3-page interactive Power BI dashboard (with Power Query for the last mile of cleaning) to answer that brief for the Commissioner's office.

KEY FINDINGS
  • Critical violations account for 54.4% of all violations citywide — more than half of all inspections uncover a safety issue serious enough to pose an immediate health hazard.
  • The top 5 violation types — equipment maintenance, pest harborage, food-contact sanitation, and temperature control — point to systemic operational gaps, not one-off issues.
  • 76.3% of graded inspections still earn a Grade A, but Chinese cuisine restaurants have the lowest Grade A rate (63.7%) among major cuisines.
  • Bangladeshi, Pakistani, and Continental cuisines carry the highest critical-violation rates citywide, all above 59%.
  • Citywide critical-violation rates climbed from 33.3% in 2015 to roughly 53% in 2024–25 — a 19-point rise, peaking near 56% during the pandemic.
  • Flushing, Queens is the single riskiest neighborhood citywide (59.5% critical rate); 4 of the 10 riskiest zip codes overall sit in Staten Island.
RECOMMENDATIONS
  • Prioritize enforcement on Bangladeshi, Pakistani, and Continental establishments, the highest-risk cuisines citywide.
  • Deploy a concentrated inspection task force to Flushing, Queens — the single riskiest neighborhood, combining the highest rate with very high volume.
  • Extend inspection frequency across Staten Island broadly, since its risk is spread across neighborhoods rather than one outlier.
  • Build cuisine-specific food-safety training for Chinese establishments, which lag on Grade A performance.
  • Track the 10-year critical-violation trend as a public KPI, targeting a return to the 2015 baseline of 33%.
